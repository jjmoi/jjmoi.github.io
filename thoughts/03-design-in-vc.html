Design in VC
250420

I did not plan to end up in venture capital. In fact, no one can plan for it, not unless you are rich enough to start your own fund. People ask me all the time how to get into VC as a designer. Same answer as any Partner level role actually. You need to know the right people. Mostly investors, aka the rich people. Then it might happen. Designers are just far more rare. There are fewer than 100 people on Earth who have held the title Design Partner. That was my job.

I was Head of Design at a successful unicorn startup that nobody had heard of, because the work lived in unsexy domains. Biotech government, defense, materials, all the boring things, you name it. If I am doing it right, the work should not make a sound (and only quitely mint money).

The company got acquired for billions of dollars. The founders moved on to start VC funds. They pulled me in as a founding partner. It was all luck, but it was also earned. It wouldn't happen if I suck at what I do.

The best part of the job is getting to design new ventures with brilliant founders from scratch. Before the org chart even exists. Investors also question me if design belongs that early. As biased as I am, I will say no. Not unless you want an unfair advantage.

You can make great products that people want. But design helps make products people desire. Design should be equal to technology. Design equals technology. It turns possibilities into something real. Something people can use., something people can believe in. When tech gets easier to assemble and the bar to ship gets lower, differentiation shifts to experience. Consumer products reset expectations for what good feels like. Now users demand that same clarity everywhere, including the unsexy parts of industry.

A Design Partner in VC is not a designer per se. Honestly, it is not a designer at all. It is a hybrid of investor, analyst, recruiter, advisor, and sometimes just a powerful friend. To be honest, it did not make me a better designer. It did not make me more marketable as a designer either. Instead I learned about investing and fundraising and running a company. You know how people say great designers need to understand business? Cool. I now understand how a business runs end to end. How investors think and what they look for, how founders raise money, how to build teams. Turns out, none of that is particularly desirable for a design hire. I learned so much about business but it is irrelevant for a designer. What people are trying to say is actually, hey you why don't you go design to make us more money, huh?

So what do I do day to day, you may ask? I work upstream. I perform due diligence on thousands of potential investments a year, focusing on the design aspects of the business. We help portfolio companies build first prototypes. We make an absurd number of pitch decks. I also got to incubate several startups myself, then go find the founders to run them. In a very real sense, I was the founder of a lot of companies before we tap someone to be the official founders.

And after that, just like a design advisor/consultant, I help founders clarify what they are building. I shape narrative and positioning so teams can decide faster. A good internal story becomes a decision making machine. Yeah, the job also includes doing branding and logos, and sometimes a request like a logo for an investor's daughter's coffee shop. Then we translate it into product reality. The first user. The first workflow. The first proof of concept. The first prototype. The first bit of culture. You know, the usual startup things.

Another great thing about this job is the power and authority that comes with it. When you hold the cash, people listen (I did not abuse it tho, for the record). I could pick only the fun problems to work on. I could say no to the boring stuff. I could shape the design vision and founders would treat it like truth.

I got my hands on projects I would never have touched otherwise. A nuclear power plant. A hotel in space. A floating city for the Waterworld timeline. It is like running a venture design studio, except I can pick the projects and never worry about customers or payroll. And when the fun dies, I tap my network and replace myself with trusted designer friends. I want my best peers in the ecosystem. I want the portfolio to keep getting better.

Sidenote: BTW, you might notice typos and a broken sentences in this writing piece. Fun fact, contrary to popular belief, investors write like dogshit. The shiny memos are written by Ivy MBA analysts. Investors write like they text their mom. Because they can.

If you are building frontier tech in AI, defense, biotech, govtech, or just anything truly cool and interesting, we should meet. Pre hype. Pre seed. Pre whatever. I want to be an early believer and one of the first angel checks. If you are here to transform billions of lives, just holler.
