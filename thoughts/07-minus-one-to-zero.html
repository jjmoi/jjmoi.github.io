Minus One to Zero
260714

Topics
startups

If zero is the commitment to your brilliant idea, everything before that is minus one, the span between noticing something and being willing to be wrong about it in front of other people. Almost everything written about early-stage building assumes you are already past it, so founders arrive at day one with a premise nobody has stress-tested and an elaborate plan for testing everything downstream of it.

Nothing shippable comes out of that span, which is why treating it as artifact work fails so reliably. Its output is a smaller number of live questions. A founder at minus one is unsure about forty things; a founder at zero is unsure about three and knows which three. Most early design work drives that count upward, because every screen you draw invites opinions about things that do not matter yet.

The object worth making at minus one is the cheapest one that can get a firm no to your idea from people. Prototypes and landing pages are useless here, since traffic is ambiguous and people are polite. Only money speaks the truth. The test is whether somebody other than your mother gives up something they cannot get back; reputation or cash. Short of that you are collecting encouragement, which is abundant, free, and uncorrelated with everything.

With almost no data, the filter that remains is taste, and taste this early works subtractively. You know within seconds that a direction is wrong long before you can say what is right, and that reaction is the most useful instrument you own. Minus one runs closer to elimination than to search: you remove the versions you would be embarrassed to ship until what is left is small enough to commit to.

But minus one also has to end, and the characteristic failure is doing it forever. Research is a comfortable place to hide, because nothing there can be falsified on a schedule and reading one more paper always looks responsible. It is extremely hard where you're at in your journey. There is no clear line to tell you than you are at zero or you have reached one yet.

The false negative is the hole in all of this, and it is a serious one. A discipline built on producing cheap nos will kill ideas that are true but early, the ones that needed somebody irrationally committed for two years before the first person would pay a cent. Any method tuned to kill bad ideas quickly kills slow good ones too, and I have no correction for it. I have used the argument above to talk myself out of things I still think about, and I cannot tell you which of those was judgment and which was pure cowardice.

The stakes are rising for a mechanical reason. As generation makes the zero-to-one stretch cheaper, the scarce work moves backward into minus one work deciding what deserves to exist at all. Choosing was always the leveraged part. It is becoming the only expensive part. Minus one to zero is the only stretch where being wrong is still free, and it does not end when the funding arrives.
